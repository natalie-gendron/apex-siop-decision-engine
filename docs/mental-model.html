The mental model behind the Apex SIOP engine — what each sidebar knob touches, and what it can never touch.

3 — Every page is a cell of world × response

response = nothing response = package
world = base The standing outlook — the default view of the business. Base vs what we will do — is the package worth it even if nothing goes wrong?
world = scenario Stress test, unmitigated — how bad does it get if we do nothing? The recommended response under that world — the package priced where it matters.

The sidebar sets the tuple (world, response) once — scenario picker, confidence knob, package multiselect — and every named view in the app is one of these four cells. Demand confidence rides along on the world axis in all of them.

4 — The words, precisely

term means is not
Forecast The estimate of demand not yet booked — the forward-looking, uncertain slice of the demand plan. Booked orders. In standard S&OP practice orders consume the forecast; inside the demand time fence only orders count as demand.
Backlog Booked orders not yet shipped or recognized — actual demand on the books ("firm" in the booking sense). A certainty — push-outs and cancellations still touch it. Nor the past-due backlog, which always carries its qualifier: in the real order book, past-due is the slice of backlog behind schedule; in the engine it is an outcome — simulated demand (forecast included) not yet served by its requested month.
Demand plan The unconstrained demand statement: forecast + backlog, customer × family × month, at requested dates. A supply commitment — it is never capped by capacity, and confidence never rewrites it.
Plan (of record) The frozen revenue commitment for the cycle, derived from the demand plan by the baseline supply plan. The yardstick behind every "vs plan" delta and every P(plan). Industry mapping: the revenue line of the AOP, and the internal number behind quarterly street guidance. A forecast. It is a promise, not a prediction — and it never moves inside a cycle. Nor guidance itself: guidance is the external, usually more conservative derivative; the engine anchors on the internal commitment, or P(plan) quietly becomes P(guidance).
Outlook The simulated outcome distribution for a context — what the demand plan, pushed through supply and calibrated by evidence, is expected to produce. Always named by its context: the base outlook (base, no response), a scenario outlook (unmitigated world), the conditioned outlook (world + response). Drawn as the solid line (center) and band. The plan (the outlook moves with the context; the plan never does — P(plan) is the measured gap). Nor "the decided version": decidedness is a sign-off, not a kind of line — whichever context the meeting commits, package or none, becomes the decision of record.
Baseline supply plan "the baseline" The feasible allocation of the demand plan across sites and months — respecting components, capacity and integration — whose revenue becomes the plan of record. "Deterministic" describes how it is computed (zero shocks), not what it is. The base case. The base outlook is a simulation; the baseline is an allocation — frozen mechanics, shown only as reference.
Targets Annual financial goals: gross margin, inventory — the margin and inventory lines of the AOP. The plan — same commitment culture, different objects. Not a synonym for "AOP": the AOP's revenue line is the plan of record, so naming only these AOP would split the term.
Scenario world An exogenous hypothesis about what happens to the business. A decision — it carries no cost, ever.
Demand Confidence world Assessed evidence of how uncertain today's world already is; calibrates demand variance, push-out and cancellation odds around the demand plan. The sidebar shorthand "trust in the forecast" names the least certain slice; the mechanics act on the whole demand plan. A hypothesis, a toggle, or a supply adjustment — all three of its knobs are demand-side, and it applies to every simulation by default.
Response package What the business chooses to do: costed, latency-ramped actions from the claim-sheet catalog. Each action carries a decision cost; package costs sum and are charged to Q1 operating income, so every EV is quoted net of cost. Part of any world — the response is the only axis where money is spent.
Decision of record The committed outcome of the cycle: the (world, response) context the meeting signs — package or none — with its conditioned outlook and cost. Today it is captured as the export's Decision of Record sheet; the planned sign-off loop makes it a first-class state and rolls it into the next cycle's plan of record. An outlook. Every outlook on screen is a candidate; this term is deliberately reserved for the one the meeting signs.

Aligned with standard S&OP usage (ASCM/APICS): a forecast excludes booked orders, the demand plan is the unconstrained forecast-plus-backlog statement of total demand, and the plan of record plays the "one set of numbers" role the meeting commits to.
